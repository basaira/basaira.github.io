Why this matters

01

Translation becomes the middleman

If every word must pass through another language first, spontaneous Arabic becomes harder to build.

02

Vocabulary stays passive

Knowing a list of translated words is not the same as recognizing and using them inside real Arabic patterns.

03

Grammar arrives before language intuition

Heavy explanation can overwhelm beginners before they have enough spoken and visual Arabic to make the structure meaningful.

FAQ

Common questions about the Arabic path

Does “through Arabic” mean beginners get no help?

It means the teaching aims to connect meaning directly to Arabic through visual, contextual and spoken cues instead of making translation the main learning mechanism.

What if I already know the Arabic letters?

The assessment helps avoid unnecessary repetition. You can begin from the stage that matches your reading, pronunciation, vocabulary and speaking ability.

Is the full long-term methodology still available?

Yes. The acquisition page is intentionally concise, while the academy site preserves the full stages, methodology and academic depth.

Do I need an account to take the first step?

No. You can request the assessment directly; student accounts belong to the student journey after enrollment.

Do not choose “beginner” by guesswork. Start from your real Arabic level.

The assessment is the bridge between your current ability and the right direct-method learning path.
